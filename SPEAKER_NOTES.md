### Slide 1 — Title: Evolving Agentic Workflows
Don't intro yourself. Hook first. Design thinking evolved from DD to Stingray. Agentic AI is the next step. No playbook yet, so I built mine into a product.

### Slide 2 — The Shift: How many people does it take?
Who it's for (1:1 personal → 1:Few niche → 1:Many scalable), each on a solo-to-full-XFN scale. Then (2016): bigger reach needed a bigger team. Now (2026): agentic AI lets one person reach almost any scale almost alone. The gap is widest for scalable products. Possible interactive moment: animate the 2016 markers sliding to 2026.

### Slide 3 — The Lineage: Design has always been a loop
DD: classic, 1990s. Stingray: tighter, more adaptive. ProtoVibing: agents per job, remove yourself from the repetitive parts. Each was the right tool for its moment.

### Slide 4 — The Trap: Why not just vibe-code it?
The trap: vibe-coding straight to a "solution" feels like idea → prompt → working app with customers, but really runs longer and grimmer: build on guesses, brittle and insecure, nobody wanted it, rebuild, out of runway. Proof of over-building: $29.5B wasted on features rarely or never used; only 6.4% see regular use (Pendo). Lesson: build less, build only what gets used. Sets up why the loop matters next.

### Slide 5 — The Loop (ProtoVibing)
Show the loop, not a list. Each output feeds the next. Idea sharpens hypothesis. Plan maps the market. Create scopes the build. Test sets OKRs. Synthesize closes the loop.

Gardening, not architecture: process over fixed blueprint, plant, tend, prune. Jazz metaphor: musicians cut dozens of songs to land a ten-track album; the discards are the point. Get comfortable iterating and throwing away bad ideas. (That's me at a jazz show last year.)
